# Gadget child-window getters at 0x0047A520..0x0047A7A0

Six 23-byte getters share one shape: `mov ecx,[esp+4]; call winGetUserData; mov eax,[eax+N]; ret` with a null guard. They differ only in `N`, and each is its own retail body.

| body | reads | ILT | identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0x0047A540` | `+0x1C` | `0x0000EE6C` | `GadgetListBoxGetUpButton` |
| `0x0047A560` | `+0x20` | `0x0001898F` | `GadgetListBoxGetDownButton` |
| `0x0047A520` | `+0x24` | `0x00044B5C` | `GadgetListBoxGetSlider` |
| `0x0047A760` | `+0x24` | `0x000123E1` | combo box, drop-down button |
| `0x0047A7A0` | `+0x28` | `0x0000DA7B` | combo box, edit box |
| `0x0047A780` | `+0x2C` | `0x0001CFAD` | combo box, list box |

The decisive evidence is the combo-box branch of retail `createGadget` (`0x00486B10`), which follows Zero Hour's `GameWindowManagerScript.cpp` order: drop-down button, edit box, list box, then that list box's up button, down button and slider. Retail calls `0x000123E1`, `0x0000DA7B` and `0x0001CFAD` on the new combo box, keeps the result of `0x0001CFAD` in `ebp` (`0x004870BD`), and passes `ebp` to `0x0000EE6C`, `0x0001898F` and `0x00044B5C` (`0x004870FB`, `0x00487133`, `0x0048716B`). So `0x0047A780` returns the list box, and the three bodies at `0x0047A540`, `0x0047A560` and `0x0047A520` are list-box getters. The list-box branch (`0x00486E5E` onwards) calls the same three on a list box.

`GadgetComboBoxGetEditBox` and `GadgetComboBoxGetDropDownButton` were claimed on `0x0047A520` and `0x0047A560` at Zero Hour's ComboBoxData offsets (`+0x24`, `+0x20`). Those bodies take a list box, so both rows are retired. Retail reads the combo-box children 4 bytes later than Zero Hour, so BFME's ComboBoxData has one more field before `dropDownButton`; placing the combo-box names on `0x0047A760`, `0x0047A7A0` and `0x0047A780` needs that field in the shim header and is a separate change.
