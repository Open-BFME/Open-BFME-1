1. `0x0016AEB0`. Six of its nine callers are the four
   `AIUpdateInterface::privateFollowWaypointPath*` variants,
   `privateAttackFollowWaypointPath` and `joinTeam`, six of the eight Zero
   Hour functions that call `getStateMachine()->setGoalWaypoint(...)`
   (`AIUpdate.cpp`; the other two are `privateWander` and `privatePanic`).
   The remaining three callers are address-named. Its ILT `0x00036192` is
   pinned as
   `AIStateMachine::setGoalWaypoint`, decoded from those call sites. Zero
   Hour calls `AIGuardMachine::setGuardMode` only from the guard states.
2. `0x0026EDC0`. Three of its eight callers are Zero Hour sites of
   `ai->setDesiredSpeed(...)`: `AIInternalMoveToState::onEnter`,
   `AIAttackPursueTargetState::updateInternal` and
   `AIFollowWaypointPathState::onEnter`. The other five are BFME or
   address-named states. No Zero Hour state calls `setGoalWaypoint`.
   In `AIInternalMoveToState::onEnter` (0x00172600, +0x5CF) the argument is
   `push 0x497423F0`, which is 999999.0f, Zero Hour's `FAST_AS_POSSIBLE`.
   Its ILT `0x00048CA7` is pinned as `AIUpdateInterface::setDesiredSpeed`.
3. `0x001BD780` has no named caller. Retail has no identical-COMDAT folding
   (`tiny-accessor-aliases.md`), so `setDesiredSpeed` cannot also live
   there; which of `Poke_Texture`, `setLastFrameInGame` and `setRiverStart`
   owns the body is not settled here.

The new rows compile from focused translation units, because the views in
`AIStates.cpp` place both fields at their Zero Hour offsets.
