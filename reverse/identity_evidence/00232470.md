# 0x00232470 copy constructor: the 16-byte record, not RectClass

The 31-byte thiscall body at 0x00232470 (ret 4) copies four dwords from its
argument to ECX and returns ECX. It was a matched row named
`??0RectClass@@QAE@ABV0@@Z`, sourced from VideoPlayer.cpp, and had no
evidence column. Commit aa06d121cc then pinned its ILT 0x0003535F as
`??0BfmeRva44E60Record@@QAE@ABU0@@Z`, which gave one body two names. Retail
was linked without identical-COMDAT folding, so only one of them can be right.

- tools/callers_of.py finds exactly one caller, including callers that go
  through the ILT: the matched Rva00233F30::rva002350c0 at 0x002350C0.
- That body default-constructs its local with 0x00233C80 (ILT 0x00033D39).
  0x00233C80 stores zero at +0x0 and at +0xC and nothing else. The same
  constructor builds the elements of the +0x12C record vector that
  Rva00244E60::add pushes. The body fills the local and returns it by value.
  `return local;` copy-constructs through the copy constructor of the local's
  own type, and the one call to 0x00232470 is that copy.
- RectClass cannot be that type. rect.h declares `RectClass( void ) {};`,
  which is empty, so it cannot be the zero-storing 0x00233C80.

The body is therefore the record type's copy constructor. The record type
keeps its established opaque name, BfmeRva44E60Record, the name its default
constructor, its vector _M_insert_overflow and its _Construct pins already
use, and the row is claimed from the TU that returns it. Probe: 31/31 exact.
