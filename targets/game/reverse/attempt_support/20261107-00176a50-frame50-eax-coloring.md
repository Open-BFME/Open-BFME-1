## What fixed the 24 bytes

1. **Escape analysis (family d, callee visibility).** The bank called the
   coordinate helpers through opaque member pointers. The fix uses WWMath's
   `class Coord3D` and gives visible `inline __declspec(noinline)` bodies to
   all six coordinate callees: `length` 0xB6CA0, `normalize` 0xFB930,
   `Scale` 0x14FFD0, `Add` 0xEC6F0, `operator-=` 0x16A240 and `operator+=`
   0x148730. VC7.1 then proves that `direction` never escapes, so:
   - the frame drops to 0x1c, with one 12-byte direction slot;
   - the BFME 2 donor shape (`direction = *victim->getUnitDirectionVector2D();`
     reusing the outer local) gives retail's interleaved copy and x87
     schedule, so the old forceinline helper is no longer needed;
   - the onEnter-style `Coord3D direction(*pos); direction.Sub2D(prev);
     direction.z = 0;` matches.

   A 64-way visibility matrix (`matrix.py`) shows the effect is all or
   nothing. If any one of the six bodies is opaque, the frame returns to 0x28
   with 56 diffs.
2. **Copy form (family c).** Two copies, `m_goalPosition = position54` in the
   horde and else paths, must be plain this-relative block copies:
   `*(CoordWords*)&m_goalPosition = *(const CoordWords*)&position54`. Any
   inlined user `operator=` (Raw, memberwise or memcpy) serialises the second
   load behind the first store. Retail exports `??4Coord3D@@QAEAAV0@ABV0@@Z`
   at 0x7E5990, and that body has the shape of an implicit block copy. The
   cleaner fix is therefore probably for the shared `coord3d.h` to stop
   declaring copy-assignment, but that is a header-wide change and was not
   tested.
3. **fld order (family f, CSE).** `TheAI->m_data->field90 + data->field94`
   gives retail's `fld [+0x90]; fadd [+0x94]`. Here `data` is a pointer local
   loaded before `length()`. Every same-base spelling, in either operand order
   (with or without a local, as an array, a nested struct, a base class, a
   double cast, a `sum +=` form, or a `>` or `!>=` comparison), emits
   `fld 94` first.

## Remaining mismatch (2 bytes)

```
retail 00ae 8b 42 3c  mov eax,[edx+0x3c] ; 00b2 89 45 50  mov [ebp+0x50],eax
ours   00ae 8b 52 3c  mov edx,[edx+0x3c] ; 00b2 89 55 50  mov [ebp+0x50],edx
```
This is the `frame50 = TheGameLogic->m_field3C;` store at the join after the
forceRepath and throttle test.

Things that do put the value in EAX:
- `TheGameLogic->getFrame()`, the ZH and EA spelling, whether
  inline or `__forceinline`, const or not, member or free function;
- a named local, block-scoped or not;
- an inline setter.

Every one of these also re-colours the scratch registers for the rest of the
function. The result is always the same 364-diff, 1040-byte body: the copy at
+0x102, the copy at +0x128, the TheAI load at +0x184 (`mov edx,[TheAI]`, one
byte longer) and later blocks all rotate among eax, ecx and edx.

Combined with that change, none of these restored retail:
- getters for the owner, goal, blocked, path, wait and aiData;
- the donor forceRepath flow, `return false` in place of `goto fail`;
- word copies or `operator=` at every copy site, and memberwise `operator=`;
- dropping the `weapon` or `data` locals, or adding variables (goal, elapsed,
  dir);
- `/Ob2`, `/G6`, `/Gy`, `/GF` (diagnostic only).

Inert without the getter:
- frame50 as `int`, a cast, a by-reference getter;
- a branch-inverted or split if, the donor flow, declaration order;
- `/Ob1` itself.

Clearly worse:
- declaring forceRepath late (60 diffs);
- `now` locals before the test (191 and 680 diffs);
- assigning in both arms (648 diffs).
