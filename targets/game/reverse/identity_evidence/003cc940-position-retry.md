## Retry hypothesis and its tests

The earlier count-temporary and return-storage blockers had a new experiment: preserve the observed signed count conversions, add field-wise copying to the existing opaque result type, and evaluate the second neighbor's weight addition before its offset subtraction. Retail duplicates the count at +0x59, adds at +0x5B and subtracts at +0x5D. Its return stores X and Z through x87 and Y through an integer register. The saved trivial result instead preserved X and Y in callee-saved registers and copied Z as integer bits. The hypothesis would be refuted if field-wise result copying and the changed count lifetime failed to reproduce those instruction sequences or improve the saved measurement.

The saved bank measured 445 bytes and 117 differences at the explicit 437-byte extent. Field-wise result copying reproduced the return sequence. Removing the second count's named float temporary restored the EDX pointer copy for the following Y count, producing the 439-byte candidate with 106 differences. The final bank forward-declares the canonical `TerrainLogic` singleton and uses the canonical float, float, coordinate-normal pointer argument widths in an opaque virtual view. This declaration also rotates the terrain call's scratch registers relative to the initial improvement; its complete probe and failing gate retain that evidence.

Rejected shapes are preserved in `measurements.json` and their numbered source and probe files: reversing the first count assignments, reversing X declarations, grouping weights or offsets, both assignment-chain directions, shared or per-branch first-count float temporaries, double X weight or offset, offset-first diagonal updates, pair storage in both field orders, compound normalization or a named fraction, and inline integer or float count accessors. The generated pointer-copy alternatives were identical to each other and worse than the selected candidate. Their source and JSON evidence are under `build/shape_search/b4e0c03c549b4496b5411d72940f5abc/`; the raw probes are `026-family-0-probe.log` and `026-family-1-probe.log`. No further unchanged variants of these families are justified.

## Constants, remaining mismatch and reopening condition

`constants.log` reads the actual four-byte float at VA 01097120, four-byte zero at VA 01075350, and eight-byte double at VA 010EE0F0. The reconstructed literals reproduce those bytes. No constant address or function pin was invented.

The first remaining operand mismatch is the X accumulator at +0x5C: retail adds to ST(3), while the candidate adds to ST(2); the following subtract also uses the other stack position. This reversal persists through the diagonal updates and the X-weight comparison. The candidate needs an extra `fxch st(1)` at +0x14D. It therefore remains two bytes longer, and the Y computation and terrain call are displaced. The canonical const dispatch view also leaves EAX and EDX exchanged in the terrain argument and vtable loads. These are unresolved compiler-shape blockers, not unresolved boundaries, callee targets or a reason to invent an owner identity. Reopening requires independently supported source lifetime or type evidence that predicts the X accumulator order, or a finite new source lever that changes the measured instruction stream at that divergence. Renaming variables, repeating the recorded temporary and declaration-order variants, or relying on normalized instruction agreement does not justify reopening.
