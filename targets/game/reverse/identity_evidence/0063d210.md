# BuddyThreadClass::Thread_Function is 0x0063EB00, not 0x0063D210

The matched 2654-byte body at 0x0063D210 carried the name
`?Thread_Function@BuddyThreadClass@@UAEXXZ`. The retail image contradicts it:

- The matched constructor `??0BuddyThreadClass@@QAE@PAVBFMENetworkLock@@@Z`
  (0x0063C4F0) installs vtable VA 0x01118E60. Its slots read from the retail
  image are: VA 0x0043DB18 (ILT, dtor), VA 0x00DDB650
  (`?Execute@ThreadClass@@UAEXXZ`, RVA 0x009DB650), then VA 0x0043CAD8. The
  slot-2 ILT 0x0003CAD8 is `jmp 0x0063EB00`. That address is the
  Thread_Function override, and its body is the Zero Hour request loop:
  getRequest and addResponse on TheGameSpyBuddyMessageQueue, and an 11-case
  jump table in Zero Hour case order that calls gpConnect, gpDeleteProfile,
  gpDisconnect, and so on.
- The matched `?callbackWrapper@@YAXPAVGPConnection@@PAX1@Z` (0x0063EA60)
  calls ILT 0x0000BEE2 at +0x36. That is the matched
  `?errorCallback@BuddyThreadClass@@QAEXPAVGPConnection@@PAUGPErrorArg@@@Z`
  thunk row, and it is `jmp 0x0063D210`. So 0x0063D210 is the errorCallback
  body, as in Zero Hour `callbackWrapper`, which dispatches `CALLBACK_ERROR`
  to `thread->errorCallback`.

The errorCallback name is already held by the 5-byte ILT row at 0x0000BEE2.
This change therefore parks 0x0063D210 under its address as
`?dup_0063D210@@YAXXZ` and does not assert a second errorCallback claim. The
source, the extent and the object-symbol alias are unchanged, and the bytes
are identical. The freed Thread_Function name goes to the clean C++ body at
0x0063EB00 in the same commit.
