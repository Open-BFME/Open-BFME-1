## Video vector

Retail `VideoPlayer::addVideo` at RVA 0x0081D2C0 reads the begin field at 0x0130B19C, end at 0x0130B1A0, and capacity end at 0x0130B1A4. It updates the end after constructing a record and passes ECX equal to the vector start to its growth helper. `removeVideo` at RVA 0x0081CF40 updates the same end field after moving records. The count and slot queries at RVAs 0x0081C5C0 and 0x0081C5F0 subtract the pointers with a 28-byte stride. The named VideoPlayer users at RVAs 0x0081CA10, 0x0081CB30 and 0x0081CD70 traverse the same records. The global initializer at RVA 0x00C6D830 and cleanup at RVA 0x00C70C70 supply the complete vector receiver. The Zero Hour `VideoPlayer.cpp` reference uses the corresponding VecVideo container for add, remove and get operations. These facts select the existing `Rva0130B19CVideoTable` vector spelling over a separately allocated begin-pointer cell or a synthetic singleton object.

The current ledger already owns 0x0130B1A0 as `g_bfmeVideoTableEnd`, defined in `VideoPlayerAddVideo.cpp`, with an evidence file that explicitly calls it the vector's end field. The interior DIR32 names are `g_Rva0081C5C0End`, `g_bfmeEndJD` and `g_bfmeVideoTableEnd`. A whole-vector row would overlap that row. The observation needed to land the whole-vector definition is a single consistent ownership representation for the vector and its fields, followed by byte verification of all scalar readers and the vector's initializer and cleanup. A different record stride or a growth receiver that does not start at 0x0130B19C would refute the vector interpretation.

## Allocation callback, mutex and stream counter

RVA 0x0082B820 loads the callback from 0x0130B1B0, tests it for null, invokes it indirectly with no arguments, then retries malloc. The writer at RVA 0x0082B120 returns the old word and stores its argument. STLport `_alloc.h` declares `__oom_handler_type` as `void (*)()` and its `__malloc_alloc` static member with that type; its setter implements the same old-value exchange. The integer spelling does not describe the indirect-call use. An argument passed to the callback, a different calling convention, or arithmetic use incompatible with a callback would refute the proposed type.

The references to 0x0130B250 occur in the false-thread allocator guard paths at RVAs 0x0082BDC0, 0x0082DDE0, 0x0082E450 and 0x0082E4F0. Their constant-false branches are retained in the unoptimized retail code. They pass the datum's address as ECX to the mutex acquire member. The acquire body at RVA 0x0082DA10 pushes that receiver into the spin-lock worker at RVA 0x0082C760. That worker reads and updates a dword through its pointer argument, including interlocked acquisition. The neighboring true-thread mutex has its own address 0x0130B254. STLport `_alloc.c` declares the allocator guard's static `_S_lock`, and its `_threads.h` mutex storage is a volatile atomic lock cell. The game shims' empty `NodeAllocMutex` and `Gen_0082ad50` types do not prove the retail object's declared extent. Because these false-thread calls are unreachable, the worker's dword access alone does not exclude a smaller unused object followed by padding. A BFME type definition or an independently placed object allocation would settle the exact extent. A receiver offset other than zero or evidence assigning the false specialization to the neighboring mutex would refute the proposed role or specialization. `mutex-layout-reference.log` retains the upstream layout references.

Retail `_S_initialize` at RVA 0x00843990 and `_S_uninitialize` at RVA 0x00842C00 update the counter at 0x0130BD30. `sync_with_stdio` at RVA 0x00842F80, the flush wrapper at RVA 0x00843610, and the initialization wrapper at RVA 0x00843D70 read it as a count or zero test. The existing `X4Iostream.cpp` defines `long ios_base::Init::_S_count = 0`, and the vendored header declares that private member as long. The void-pointer spelling is misplaced. A pointer dereference through the loaded word or a non-counter writer would refute this conclusion.

## Numpunct string members

The RTTI-backed narrow numpunct vtable at 0x0112EAFC assigns its fourth and fifth non-destructor operations (slots 4 and 5) to RVAs 0x00844540 and 0x00844570. The wide numpunct vtable at 0x0112EB18 assigns slots 4 and 5 to RVAs 0x008445C0 and 0x008445F0. Their complete-object locators name `numpunct<char>` and `numpunct<unsigned short>` independently of the ledger's synthetic `T2StrRet` names. STLport `_numpunct.h` declares the virtual order as decimal point, thousands separator, grouping, truename and falsename, and declares the corresponding `_M_truename` and `_M_falsename` string members.

Each getter passes the indicated datum to a matching narrow or wide string copy constructor. The narrow getters call ILT VA 0x0041CB11, whose raw E9 routes to VA 0x008FB1B0; wide getters call ILT VA 0x0043050D, whose raw E9 routes to VA 0x008FB220. Startup writers at RVAs 0x00C6D9E0, 0x00C6DA10, 0x00C6DA70 and 0x00C6DAA0 construct the indicated strings from the retail literals. The narrow literals are at VAs 0x01080FB4 and 0x0111C2A0. The wide literals are at VAs 0x0112F398 and 0x0112F3A4. Raw byte reads in `final-facts.log` establish `true` and `false` in the appropriate encodings. Cleanups at RVAs 0x00C70DD0, 0x00C70DE0, 0x00C70E00 and 0x00C70E10 pass the corresponding complete string receiver to the existing destructors.

The scratch compiler probe in `member-names.log` and `member-names-size-gate.log` emits the real protected static member spellings from the existing STLport header and proves both string types occupy 12 bytes. Its objects are scratch evidence, not game definitions. `data_rows.symbol_size` still refuses each actual emitted member because `_cpp_data_access` does not admit a template scope. No access-exposing shim, alias, guessed global role name, or body identity rewrite is substituted. A vtable slot order that differs from the witnessed header, a different constructor character width, or startup literals inconsistent with the getter role would refute these names. Landing needs the canonical member spelling to be admitted by the sanctioned data-size verifier and source declarations to be reconciled without changing the verified getters or lifetime bodies.
